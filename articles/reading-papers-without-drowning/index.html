Reading Papers Without Drowning

A multi-pass approach to research papers that saves hours and helps you decide what deserves a close read.

ASRBMY Jul 31, 2026 1 min read
  • research
  • reading
Table of Contents

  2. Pass two: follow the argument
  3. Pass three: challenge it
  4. Habits that help
  5. When to stop

A single research paper can take hours to read properly, and you rarely need that for every one. Reading in passes lets you stop as soon as you have what you need.

Pass one: the five-minute skim#

Read the title, abstract, introduction and conclusion. Glance at the headings and figures. Then ask:

  1. What problem does this paper address?
  2. What do the authors claim to have found?
  3. Is it relevant enough to read further?

Many papers end here, and that is a good outcome. S. Keshav describes a three-pass method like this in his short article "How to Read a Paper"; it is worth reading in full.

Pass two: follow the argument#

Now read carefully, but skip the proofs and fine technical detail. Study the figures and tables: are the axes labelled, do the error bars exist, are the comparisons fair? Note references you want to follow up.

Pass three: challenge it#

Only for papers that matter to your work. Try to reconstruct the argument yourself and notice where you disagree. The aim is to understand the paper well enough to explain its weaknesses.

Habits that help#

  • Keep a list of questions before you start; reading with a purpose is faster.
  • Write a three-sentence summary immediately afterwards, while it is fresh.
  • Read the related-work section to learn the field, not just the paper.
  • Accept that you will not understand everything on the first read.

When to stop#

If the second pass shows the paper is not useful, stop and move on. Finishing something just because you started it is a poor use of attention.
